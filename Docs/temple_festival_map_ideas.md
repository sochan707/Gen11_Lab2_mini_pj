# Temple Festival Map Ideas

## Main idea: Festival at the Foot of the Temple

Build a small explorable loop that starts in a lively night market and climbs toward a quiet temple. The change in sound, light, and space makes reaching the temple feel meaningful. Aim for a map that takes about 5–8 minutes to explore, with the main route visible from the entrance.

```text
                    [TEMPLE / PAGODA]
                         |  steps
                 [QUIET COURTYARD]
                    /           \
          [GARDEN + POND]     [LANTERN PATH]
                    \           /
              [FESTIVAL FOOD STREET]
                 /      |       \
          [STALLS]  [PLAZA]  [STALLS]
                         |
                 [ENTRANCE GATE]
```

The two side paths form a loop, so players can explore without getting stuck at a dead end. Keep the temple visible above the stalls and stairs as a constant landmark.

## What goes in each area

1. **Entrance gate:** A clear starting point, a festival sign, and a first view of the temple roof. Put a simple map board here if players need direction.
2. **Food street:** A gently curving main path with 4–6 distinct stalls. Give each stall a recognizable silhouette or color: steaming noodles, skewers over a grill, sweets, fruit drinks, and a small seating area. Leave enough room to walk around a crowd.
3. **Central plaza:** Open space for a performance, lantern display, or food tasting activity. This is the visual and social center of the festival.
4. **Garden and pond:** A calmer optional path with a photo spot or small collectible. Use the pond, trees, and rocks to frame views back to the market.
5. **Lantern path:** A second route to the temple, narrower and more atmospheric. Place lanterns in a rhythm that naturally guides the player uphill.
6. **Temple courtyard:** A pause before the final steps. Reduce clutter and festival noise. Use symmetry, stone paving, and one strong focal point.
7. **Temple / pagoda:** The destination and reward. A short interaction, story reveal, or panoramic overlook gives players a reason to complete the walk.

## Exploration ideas

- **Food stamp trail:** Visiting three stalls earns a festival stamp or token that opens a small temple story moment. Keep the temple visit possible without completing every stall.
- **Environmental clues:** Repeat one symbol on the entrance sign, a stall decoration, and a temple carving. This gives observant players a small discovery.
- **Optional views:** Let the pond path reveal the temple from a new angle; let the temple overlook reveal the whole festival below.
- **Human scale:** Put benches, baskets, signs, and warm lights near the stalls. Use fewer props as the player approaches the temple.

## Visual and audio direction

| Area | Light and color | Sound | Feeling |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Festival | Warm lanterns, orange and red accents | Music, cooking, conversation | Busy and welcoming |
| Transition paths | Lantern pools with darker gaps | Music fades, insects or wind emerge | Curious |
| Temple | Cooler moonlight with a few warm candles | Bells, wind, distant festival | Calm and special |

## Build order in Unity

1. Block out the terrain with simple shapes: entrance, food street, plaza, two side paths, stairs, courtyard, temple.
2. Walk the entire route before decorating. Check that the temple is visible from the entrance and that both side paths connect.
3. Place large landmarks: temple, gate, stairs, mountains, pond, and stall silhouettes.
4. Add lights and color zones to make the change from festival to temple clear.
5. Add food props, signs, seating, and small exploration rewards last.

## Note on the project's assets

The project already contains pagoda, torii gate, staircase, mountain, grass, and temple texture assets. The pagoda, stairs, and mountains can anchor the first blockout. A torii is specifically associated with Japanese shrines; if the intended temple and festival draw from another culture, use a gate shape and decorations that fit that reference instead. Choose one architectural reference before final decoration so the map feels cohesive.
